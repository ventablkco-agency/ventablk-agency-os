# Website Design & Content Approval Workflow
**VentaBlk Co. — Reusable client approval process**  
**Status:** Draft for review

## Purpose

Keep design and content decisions clear, avoid untracked feedback, and make sure the client approves the right deliverable before development or launch proceeds.

## 1. Roles

- **Agency project lead:** sends review links, consolidates feedback, records decisions, tracks changes and dates.
- **Client decision-maker:** provides one consolidated set of feedback and makes final business decisions.
- **Content/clinical reviewer:** verifies hospital services, doctors, credentials, clinical claims, emergency instructions, and medical content.
- **Technical owner:** verifies feasibility, integrations, performance, accessibility, and launch requirements.

One person may fill multiple roles, but each responsibility must have a named owner.

## 2. Approval gates

### Gate A — Brief and sitemap
Client approves target audiences, conversion goals, page inventory, navigation, and content responsibilities before detailed page design.

### Gate B — Visual direction
Client reviews the visual reference, typography, colors, key components, and representative responsive behavior. Approval locks the design direction, not every final pixel.

### Gate C — Page design
Client reviews key page designs and the mobile/tablet/desktop behavior. Confirm content hierarchy, calls to action, forms, and important states. Record feedback in one consolidated list.

### Gate D — Content and factual verification
Client approves visible text, business facts, services, staff profiles, contact details, imagery, and permissions. Clinical content requires the designated qualified/authorized reviewer where appropriate.

### Gate E — Staging acceptance
Client tests the deployed preview/staging site against agreed scope. Record defects separately from new scope requests. Fix launch-blocking defects before final approval.

### Gate F — Launch approval
Client confirms content and functionality are approved for production, required access is ready, and launch timing is agreed. Do not launch based solely on an informal assumption of approval.

## 3. Review request format

Every review request should include:
- Project and review stage.
- Direct link to the exact design or staging version.
- What the client is being asked to review.
- What is in scope for this review and what is not.
- Feedback deadline and how to submit one consolidated response.
- How approval will be recorded.
- Any known issues or decisions still pending.

## 4. Feedback rules

- Ask for one consolidated feedback list from the designated decision-maker.
- Request feedback that identifies page/section, issue, desired outcome, and priority.
- Distinguish a defect against approved scope from a new feature or direction change.
- Record scope changes, timeline effects, and any extra cost in writing before proceeding.
- Keep approved decisions; do not silently overwrite them.
- If feedback conflicts, pause the affected work and ask the designated decision-maker to resolve it.
- Do not treat silence as approval unless the contract explicitly defines that process and the client has accepted it.

## 7. Definition of approval

Approval should identify the exact version and stage. A visual design approval does not automatically approve unverified medical content, legal text, payment terms, or production behavior. Staging acceptance does not replace the final launch checklist.
